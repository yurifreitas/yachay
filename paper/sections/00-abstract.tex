% ABSTRACT - the four moves: problem, what is wrong, what we did, what it changed.
Screening pipelines rank candidate entities by an aggregate score estimated from a varying
number of noisy observations. Most screening aggregates are not means but \emph{selection}
operators --- maxima, top-$k$ means, quantiles, enrichment scores --- and every such operator
is positively biased, with the bias growing as the estimate gets noisier. Where observation
counts vary across entities, the score is therefore not comparable across entities, and its
ranking is partly a ranking of who was measured least. We present \texttt{sieve}, which
corrects this empirically: resample observations known to carry no effect, apply the
\emph{same} statistic, and standardise by observation count. In a perturbation screen, the
correction showed that the celebrated training maximum of \ObesityTrainingMax{} was measured
on a single observation, where pure noise averages \ObesityNullMeanAtOne{}; dissolved a
reported viability confound of \ObesityConfoundRaw{} to \ObesityConfoundCal{}; and moved the
most important entity in the screen from rank \ObesityRankBefore{} to rank
\ObesityRankAfter{}. At scale on DepMap (\DepmapCellLines{} cell lines $\times$
\DepmapGenesScored{} genes), it exposes that \DepmapPanEssentialInRawTop10{} of the raw top
ten are known pan-essential genes. We state two measured anomalies that remain unexplained.
